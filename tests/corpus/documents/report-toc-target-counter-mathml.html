Introduction

An invoice under EN 16931 states amounts at three levels: the net amount of each line, the sum of line net amounts, and the tax amount per VAT category. Every amount must be rounded to two decimals, but the standard leaves open whether tax is computed per line and then summed, or once per category from the summed net amounts.1

The two approaches can differ by several cents on long invoices. Validators then reject documents whose totals were computed with the other method, although both are permitted by the tax law of most member states. This report quantifies the difference and gives bounds that implementations can use to decide which method a received invoice used.

Rounding models

Let an invoice have lines i = 1, …, n with net amounts ai (already rounded to cents) in one VAT category with rate t.

Line-level rounding

Tax is computed and rounded per line, then summed:

Tline=∑i=1nround(t·ai)

Document-level rounding

Net amounts are summed first and tax is rounded once:

Tdoc=round(t·∑i=1nai)

EN 16931 business rule BR-CO-17 requires that the category tax amount equals the category taxable amount multiplied by the rate, rounded to two decimals, which corresponds to document-level rounding. Many accounting systems nevertheless compute tax per line.

Bounds on the difference

Each rounding introduces an error of at most half a cent. Writing εi for the error of line i, the difference between both methods is bounded by

|Tline−Tdoc|≤∑i=1n|εi|+12≤n+12

in cents. If the errors are independent and uniformly distributed on [−½, ½], their sum has variance n/12, so the typical difference grows only with n:

E[|Tline−Tdoc|]≈n6π

Experiments

Data set

We used 52,000 invoices exported from four accounting systems, with between 1 and 640 lines each. All invoices use a single VAT category of 19 % or 7 %; invoices with several categories were split per category.

for each invoice:
    t_line = sum(round(rate * a) for a in lines)
    t_doc  = round(rate * sum(lines))
    record(len(lines), abs(t_line - t_doc))

Recommendations

Senders should compute tax at document level, as BR-CO-17 requires. Receivers should accept a difference of up to (n+1)/2 cents when they recompute totals, and flag larger differences as errors rather than rounding artefacts.2
