Optimal Line Breaking for Print-Oriented
HTML Rendering Engines

Ada Example1, Ben Sample2, Chiara Test1
1Institute for Document Engineering, Example University   2Typesetting Lab, Sample Institute

Abstract. Browsers break lines greedily: every line is filled with as many words as fit, and the remaining words move to the next line. Typesetting systems such as TeX instead choose break points that minimize a global measure of badness. We measure the difference between the two strategies on a corpus of 1,200 printed business documents, show that greedy breaking produces visibly loose lines in 7.4% of justified paragraphs, and describe how a print engine can offer optimal breaking without giving up compatibility with browser output.

Introduction

Many applications generate invoices, contracts and reports as HTML and convert them to PDF with a headless browser. The output then looks exactly like the browser preview, which is the main reason for this choice. Its typography, however, is limited by the line breaking algorithm of the browser, which is optimized for speed and incremental layout rather than for quality.

TeX’s algorithm [1] treats a paragraph as a sequence of boxes, glue and penalties and finds the break points that minimize the sum of the squared badness of all lines. This produces evenly spaced justified text, but it is more expensive and its result can change when a word at the end of the paragraph changes.

In this paper we ask how often the difference is visible in practice, and whether a print engine can use optimal breaking only where it improves the result.1

Method

We collected 1,200 documents printed by 31 applications: invoices (412), contracts and terms (288), reports (301) and letters (199). Each document was rendered with a greedy and a total-fit line breaker at the same measure, using the same fonts and hyphenation patterns.2 For every justified paragraph we recorded the adjustment ratios of all lines but the last.

Results

Greedy breaking produced at least one loose line (r > 1) in 7.4% of justified paragraphs; total-fit breaking reduced this share to 1.9%. The effect was strongest in contracts, whose numbered clauses are typeset in narrow columns with hanging indents.

The total-fit breaker changed the number of lines in 3.1% of paragraphs. In 0.4% of documents this moved content to another page, which matters when documents must match a preview exactly.

A hybrid strategy

A print engine that promises browser-identical output cannot change line breaks silently. We therefore propose a hybrid: lines are broken greedily, and only paragraphs that contain a loose line are re-broken with total-fit, under the constraint that the number of lines does not change. This keeps pagination identical while removing three quarters of the loose lines.

The constraint is cheap to enforce: the dynamic programming pass only needs to track solutions with exactly the greedy line count, which bounds the search to a narrow band around the greedy break points.3

Conclusion

Greedy line breaking is good enough for most printed documents, but a visible minority of justified paragraphs, especially in contracts, would benefit from optimal breaking. A constrained total-fit pass can improve them without changing pagination.
